Keepsake

Privacy

Signing in

You sign in with Apple, or with your email address and a six-digit code emailed to you. Your address is kept with your account. With Apple you can hide your address; Keepsake then holds the private one Apple makes for it, and your name the first time.

You can add a passkey, so that Face ID signs you in next time. Its private half stays in your iPhone’s Passwords; Keepsake’s servers hold only its public half.

Your phone stays signed in until you sign out, or for as long as you set under Stay signed in. Lock with Face ID keeps Keepsake closed until your phone knows you; Keepsake never sees your face, only your phone’s yes or no.

What Keepsake keeps

On its servers, at Supabase:

  • your email address, and the name and photo you set;
  • each keepsake you seal: its photos, video, sound, letters, songs, title and message, and how it is laid out;
  • who is on your Circle, the lines you leave each other, and the names and photos you give people, which only you see;
  • your labels, and for each keepsake whether you have archived or deleted it, where it sits, and how often and when you last opened it, which only you see;
  • your invites: when each was made, opened, used or cancelled and by whom, with a scrambled form of its code, never the code;
  • a token for each phone you allow notifications on, and which notifications you want.

On your phone: a keepsake stays there until you seal it, with copies of what you have opened and your settings.

Who sees what

  • A keepsake you seal: you and the person you send it to. One for someone new: you, and whoever takes up its invite.
  • People on your Circle: your name and photo.
  • Anyone who opens your invite, by its link or its code: your name and photo, until the invite is used, cancelled or 14 days old.
  • When you open a keepsake, its sender gets a notification saying so, unless either of you has turned that notification off.

Keepsakes are not end-to-end encrypted: whoever runs Keepsake’s servers can read them.

Other services

  • Supabase, the service Keepsake’s servers run on, keeps everything above.
  • Resend delivers the sign-in email, so it handles your address and the code.
  • If you sign in with Apple, Apple tells Keepsake it is you. Apple knows you use Keepsake.
  • Notifications pass through Expo’s push service and Apple. Each holds a name and a few words, such as “sent you a keepsake”, never what a keepsake holds.
  • To find a song, Keepsake sends the words you search for, or the link you paste, to Apple, Spotify or YouTube; the song’s artwork and preview come from them.
  • What you share leaves through your phone’s share sheet, to whoever you choose.

Keepsake has no ads, analytics or trackers.

Your phone

Keepsake asks for the camera and microphone, to record video and sound, for notifications, and for Face ID if you turn on its lock or a passkey. It reads only the photos, videos and sound files you pick, and the clipboard when you paste a song’s link.

Deleting

  • Delete for me takes a keepsake out of your view; the other person keeps theirs. Delete for Everyone removes it, and its files, for everyone who holds it.
  • You can cancel an invite before it is used, remove a line you left, and change your name and photo.
  • Signing out takes the phone off your notifications and clears Keepsake’s drafts, files and caches from it.
  • Delete my account, in Settings, removes your account and everything you hold. Keepsakes you have sent stay with the people who hold them. Your passkeys go from Keepsake’s servers; remove the one in your iPhone’s Passwords yourself. If you signed in with Apple, Keepsake also asks Apple to stop Sign in with Apple for it.
